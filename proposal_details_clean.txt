1  ·  T H E  O P P O R T U N I T Y
Joe's food, your setting, less of your time
Appley already has what most cafés pay for: a view, passing walkers and cyclists, and a chef with
a clear idea of what he wants to cook. This pilot turns that into a focused breakfast and brunch
offer, and takes the launch work off your desk.
Joe leads the food
A compact menu of quality
breakfast and brunch
dishes, built on a small set of
shared ingredients so it
stays fast and consistent on
a busy morning.
IsleConnect runs the launch

photography, posts, the
campaign page and a
weekly results summary.
You make the key decisions
Prices, spend and what gets
published. About three and
a half hours of your time
across the whole pilot.
What the pilot is designed to answer
Does a compact, well-presented breakfast and brunch menu bring in more customers during the
morning service, and at what spend?
Can the kitchen deliver it consistently at busy times, with food costs close to the costed figures?
Do walkers, cyclists and local residents respond to the campaign, and how do they hear about it?
Which outside-space improvements are worth making next, based on how customers actually use
the space?

One chef-approved record per dish
Joe approves each dish once: ingredients, portions, supplier costs, method, allergens, price and a
reference photograph. From that single record, IsleConnect's AI-assisted workflow produces the
costing, prep sheet, staff briefing card, menu wording and social copy, and flags any dish whose
cost moves when a supplier price changes. Joe keeps authority over recipes and food
information, and the owner over prices and publication. Every output is checked by a person
before use.

Improve it in stages, as demand shows
Customers come to Appley for the view, so the outside space matters. Spend in steps, and only
move to the next stage when the evidence supports it.

A pavement A-board, QR table cards that link to the menu and
campaign page, and three large planters to define the seating
area. Record which tables fill first, when, and in what weather.

Permissions to check ﬁrst
If seating, A-boards or planters sit on the public highway, Isle of Wight Council requires a pavement
licence (£150 for one year or £225 for two) and may require a street furniture permit (£225) for A-
boards and planters. Seating on the café's own leased land falls under the lease, and council-
owned park or seafront land needs the landowner's consent. IsleConnect will check the lease and
confirm the position with the council's licensing team before any outside purchases.
Costs are current UK retail and trade prices for about 24 covers, rounded to the nearest £10, mixing prices with and without VAT as
published. Supplier quotes will replace them before purchase.

The story behind it
04
Peter J Murray’s The Darker Side of Wight brings a darker edge to real Island places. Shrouded in Garlic takes that world to the farm through Matty, a young visitor who finds that garlic, light and reflection are clues to something unexplained.
